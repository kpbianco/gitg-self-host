## 06.01 — Receive one useful act of help while keeping your own voice

**Goal.** Recognize ordinary vulnerability and interdependence, express a real need, and receive bounded help without treating dependence as a loss of dignity.

**How this represents the competency.** One everyday exchange explores bodily fragility, emotional exposure, dependence and uncertainty. It does not reproduce disability or prove how someone would respond during a crisis or prolonged dependency.

**Before starting.** Choose a real, modest uncertainty such as understanding a public timetable or an unfamiliar set of ordinary instructions. Ask a willing person for clarification only if you need it. Use paper, dictation or a private note; do not create a difficulty, withhold an existing support or disclose sensitive history to make the exercise possible.

**Time and effort.** About 10 minutes to prepare, one brief exchange, and 10 minutes afterward.

### 1. Locate the need inside an ordinary network of support

Trace one normal day through five questions: what does your body need; where could misunderstanding or rejection matter; whose work or assistance makes the day possible; what remains uncertain; and what could you not provide entirely alone? Use ordinary examples such as rest, a question to a stranger, reliable transport and accessible information. Then identify the actual uncertainty selected in setup. Separate the useful statement I need this explained from a global verdict that needing help makes you incapable. Name one choice you still want to make yourself.

Observable checks:

- Five dimensions of ordinary vulnerability are considered.
- A real limited need is named.
- Dependence is separated from a judgment of worth.
- One retained choice is identified.

### 2. Ask for a specific contribution with a real option to decline

Use this request, adapted to the actual question: I am unsure which instruction applies here; would you explain that part, or is now inconvenient? State the exact part you need help with, the form that works for you, and what you will decide afterward. Let the person decline or offer a different time. Avoid apologizing for your existence, demanding reassurance or promising an unlimited return favor. If the first person cannot help, consider an appropriate information service or leave the need unresolved rather than inventing a successful exchange.

Observable checks:

- The request names the needed contribution.
- The helper can freely decline.
- The preferred form of help is communicated.
- Any agreement preserves the retained choice.

### 3. Receive the help and distinguish assistance from takeover

If help is agreed, listen or read, ask a clarifying question if needed, and use the explanation to make your own next decision. Acknowledge the specific assistance in a proportionate way. Review what became possible, what remains uncertain, and whether you or the helper took over a choice that was not offered. If an unwanted extra step was proposed, state that the agreed clarification is enough. Keep an unsuccessful request visible; preparing or asking alone does not demonstrate receiving help.

Observable checks:

- Agreed help is actually received.
- Its practical effect is identified.
- Unoffered choices remain with the person receiving help.
- Appreciation does not create unlimited obligation.

**Adaptation.** Use text, sign, an interpreter or a trusted communication partner as appropriate. Asking for accessible information is legitimate help. If no suitable exchange is available, rehearse the supplied request and label it rehearsal; defer the receiving action. No personal disclosure or performance of helplessness is required.

**Review.** What assistance was useful, which choice remained yours, and what did the exchange reveal about ordinary dependence? Decide whether the need is met, another clarification is needed, or this source of help is unsuitable. Neither receiving nor providing help establishes greater human worth.

**What the outcomes can look like:**

- **Supportive:** A person asked about an unfamiliar timetable, received a clear explanation, chose their own route and thanked the helper without promising a favor.
- **Mixed:** The explanation was useful, but the helper also began making unrelated plans; the recipient clarified what help was actually wanted.
- **Contradictory:** The recipient felt obliged to accept an unwanted decision because receiving any help was treated as surrendering the right to choose.
- **Inconclusive:** The request was drafted but no exchange occurred, so the record supports preparation rather than receiving assistance.

Minimum and full completion retain the existing action-count and explicit final-review rules. Neither means mastery of the competency.

## 06.02 — Recognize possible trauma effects and prepare a suitable support route

**Goal.** Understand possible trauma-related responses, distinguish observations from diagnoses, and prepare to seek qualified help without forcing disclosure or exposure.

**How this represents the competency.** This is literacy and support preparation using a fictional case. It is not a symptom screen, diagnosis, trauma narrative, memory-recovery exercise or treatment.

**Before starting.** Read the NIMH PTSD overview through the reference. Use this fictional case: following a frightening accident, someone startles at noises, has unwanted recollections, avoids a familiar route and finds it harder to trust others. No account of the accident is needed. Do not substitute an active personal trauma for this case.

**Time and effort.** Three sessions of up to 15 minutes, with reading split or deferred as needed.

### 1. Explain eight concepts without diagnosing the case

Make a short glossary: threat means perceived danger; avoidance means steering away from reminders; intrusion means unwelcome recollections or images; dissociation can involve feeling detached or unreal; shame concerns negative judgment of oneself; hyperarousal means being persistently on guard; memory difficulties can include gaps or trouble concentrating; attachment concerns security and trust in relationships. Match only the case's stated observations to possible concepts and mark the rest unknown. Consult the NIMH account to check the symptom-related terms. These experiences can have different causes; neither a matching word nor an absent sign decides a diagnosis.

Observable checks:

- All eight concepts receive a plain-language explanation.
- Case observations are kept separate from inferred causes.
- Unstated experiences remain unknown.
- Clinical diagnosis is reserved for a qualified assessment.

### 2. Compare a supportive response with pressure to perform recovery

Write a two-sentence response to the fictional person: You do not have to describe the accident to me. Would help finding a clinician or practical support with an ordinary task be useful? Contrast it with being told to retell the event, confront reminders alone or recover on someone else's timetable. Explain how choice, predictability and control over disclosure alter the interaction. Identify a peer's role in listening or practical help and the clinician's role in assessment and treatment. Do not test reactions to reminders or ask anyone to recount a traumatic event.

Observable checks:

- The response gives a choice about support.
- Disclosure is optional and limited.
- Pressure or unsupervised exposure is explicitly rejected.
- Peer support and clinical treatment have different roles.

### 3. Find an actual professional entry point and prepare questions

Use the linked NIMH help page or an official health-service directory for your location to locate a real route to a clinician experienced with trauma. Record the service name, public contact route, area served and what eligibility or cost information still needs checking. Prepare these questions: how do you assess fit; how is consent handled during treatment; and what choices are available if a method is unsuitable? No appointment or disclosure is required for this preparation. If there is a current urgent concern, use qualified or emergency help now rather than finishing the exercise; in the US, 988 offers crisis support and 911 is for life-threatening emergencies. Elsewhere use local services.

Observable checks:

- A real service has a retrievable contact route.
- Eligibility and unknown access conditions are recorded.
- Questions address expertise consent and alternatives.
- Preparation is distinguished from obtaining assessment or treatment.

**Adaptation.** Use a short spoken glossary or a reader's help with public information. Skip distressing detail and retain the fictional case. This is not exposure practice; a professional can advise on appropriate personal work. If the topic is destabilizing, stop and seek suitable support rather than recording endurance as success.

**Review.** Can you explain what might be happening without claiming to know its cause, and identify a usable next support step? Preserve unanswered access questions. Success here is informed preparation and appropriate boundaries, not reduced symptoms or demonstrated recovery.

**What the outcomes can look like:**

- **Supportive:** The case was described without a diagnosis, missing experiences stayed unknown, and a local clinical entry point and consent questions were identified.
- **Mixed:** The glossary was accurate but the selected service had unclear eligibility, so access remained a question to resolve.
- **Contradictory:** A learner declared PTSD from the short case or proposed forcing a return to the feared location as a test of progress.
- **Inconclusive:** Symptom words were copied but neither their meaning nor a real professional entry point was established.

**Reference support:**

- [Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd) — Trauma-related experiences can involve unwanted recollections, avoidance, heightened arousal, detachment and changes in mood or cognition; diagnosis and treatment require an appropriate mental health professional. Supports literacy and professional boundaries, not diagnosis from a short case, a screening score, forced exposure or the effectiveness of the product-authored exercise. The attachment concept is a broader educational category rather than an added diagnostic criterion.
- [Help for Mental Illnesses](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/find-help) — US life-threatening emergencies call for 911; 988 provides suicide and emotional-crisis support by call or text, and ordinary mental health care requires an appropriate provider. US public resource guidance only. Local availability, accessibility, eligibility and other countries' services need current verification. A resource card or rehearsal is not a clinical assessment or emergency-response qualification.

Minimum and full completion retain the existing action-count and explicit final-review rules. Neither means mastery of the competency.

## 06.03 — Build a choice-preserving support plan after a violation

**Goal.** Identify protective supports and survivor-chosen steps that address safety, agency, trust, bodily ownership, boundaries and meaning without blaming the harmed person.

**How this represents the competency.** The supplied case supports planning and response rehearsal. It does not demonstrate recovery from assault, abuse, coercion or betrayal, and it does not authorize investigating harm or confronting a suspected perpetrator.

**Before starting.** Use this fictional case: a trusted person shared private correspondence after repeatedly pressuring its owner; the owner wants privacy and support but does not want physical comfort or a detailed retelling. Do not invent additional violence. Read RAINN's explanation of its approach to choice and support as one provider's example, not as a service for every kind of betrayal.

**Time and effort.** About 20 minutes for the plan, a short private rehearsal, and 10 minutes to check a support route.

### 1. Give each area of recovery a concrete protective option

Create six entries for the fictional person: safety may require advice about further access to private material; agency means choosing the next step; trust can begin with a limited agreed task; bodily ownership means asking before touch; boundaries define what information may be shared; meaning can include an ordinary valued activity without a redemptive explanation. For each, write what the person can choose and what a supporter must ask before doing. Separate the violator's responsibility from the harmed person's options now. Safety planning for ongoing abuse belongs with an appropriate trained service, not a generic instruction to confront or abruptly leave.

Observable checks:

- Six recovery areas have concrete protective options.
- Choices remain with the harmed person.
- Harm is not attributed to insufficient resilience or prevention.
- Ongoing danger calls for appropriate professional safety support.

### 2. Rehearse support without taking control of the account

Privately practice this opening: What happened to your privacy matters; you can choose what to tell me. Would you prefer company, practical help finding support, or space? Then rehearse the person declining touch, declining details and accepting help finding a service. Respond to each choice directly. Do not add a demand to forgive, reconcile, report, explain a lesson or prove credibility to you. State that confidentiality has limits where immediate safety or applicable reporting duties require help; avoid promises you cannot keep. No message is sent and no real person's story is solicited for the rehearsal.

Observable checks:

- The opening acknowledges the particular violation.
- Three different preferences receive respectful responses.
- Forgiveness reporting and reconciliation are not imposed.
- Confidentiality is described without an absolute promise.

### 3. Make one protective support option usable

Choose one need from the plan and locate a relevant public service: a survivor-support organization for sexual violence, an abuse-support service for coercion, a qualified clinician, or an appropriate privacy or advocacy service for the supplied case. Check what it actually offers, who can use it and how someone can ask about privacy before sharing details. Add a draft request limited to the help wanted. For an actual manageable situation, any contact is the affected person's choice and may require a safer device or channel advised by the service. A saved public route is preparation; it is not a completed recovery step for someone else.

Observable checks:

- The service fits the specific support need.
- Its scope and access conditions are checked.
- The draft request discloses only necessary information.
- The record distinguishes preparation from chosen live contact.

**Adaptation.** Keep the exercise fictional or work with a qualified support person. Use a neutral written response if speech or role-play is uncomfortable. Do not ask a survivor to supply training material, remove their supports or document private evidence here. Immediate danger requires local emergency help rather than finishing this plan.

**Review.** Which concrete choice does each part of the plan protect? Where could helpful intent become pressure or takeover? Keep unresolved safety and access questions visible, and name the next appropriate support step without assigning a recovery deadline.

**What the outcomes can look like:**

- **Supportive:** The plan covered privacy, bodily choice and trust, and the rehearsal accepted a refusal of touch while identifying an appropriate support route.
- **Mixed:** The supporter respected the person's wishes but initially selected a service outside its stated scope; the mismatch was recorded and corrected.
- **Contradictory:** Help was made conditional on reporting, reconciliation or a detailed account, or blame was placed on the person whose boundary was violated.
- **Inconclusive:** The plan contained general encouragement but no specific choice, protective option or usable support route.

**Reference support:**

- [Help and Healing](https://rainn.org/help-and-healing/) — RAINN describes survivor support that prioritizes safety, trust, autonomy and choices without pressure; it recognizes that recovery follows different paths. A sexual-violence support provider's description of its approach, not empirical validation of the exercise or a claim that this service handles every kind of betrayal. No treatment method or published support mnemonic is reproduced.

Minimum and full completion retain the existing action-count and explicit final-review rules. Neither means mastery of the competency.

## 06.04 — Plan a meaningful day with room for changing capacity

**Goal.** Adapt ordinary commitments around fluctuating symptoms, treatment and uncertainty while communicating limits and preserving valued identity beyond illness.

**How this represents the competency.** This is practical scheduling and communication, not a treatment or exercise prescription. A fictional plan demonstrates preparedness; personal adaptation requires an actual usable trial within existing care guidance.

**Before starting.** Use an ordinary upcoming day if you already manage variable capacity, or this fictional day: a person has an established care appointment, a household task and a valued conversation, but cannot predict how much energy will be available. Do not invent a diagnosis or alter care. Read the CDC ME/CFS reference for a specific reason that pushing activity can be unsuitable; its disease-specific guidance is not universal.

**Time and effort.** About 20 minutes to prepare, one ordinary day or fictional walk-through, and a short later review that allows delayed effects to be noticed.

### 1. Write a day plan that can change without becoming a failure

List essential care commitments under the existing plan, necessary daily needs and one personally meaningful connection or interest. For each ordinary activity, describe a usual form, a reduced form and a way to defer or ask for help. In the supplied case, the conversation could be a short text instead of a visit; the household task could be delegated rather than caught up later. Keep the care appointment or ask the service how to adjust it instead of deciding medical priorities yourself. Leave room for uncertainty rather than assigning an invented energy score or promising what a good morning will permit.

Observable checks:

- Existing care commitments are preserved or referred to the care team.
- Ordinary activities have reduced or supported alternatives.
- A valued interest or relationship remains visible.
- The plan allows uncertainty without invented capacity scores.

### 2. Communicate the limit before an obligation silently expands

Prepare a concrete message for one affected person: I would like to stay connected; today I can offer a short message, and I cannot commit to a visit. Can we use that form and revisit the plan later? State the limit, the valued purpose and the offered alternative without supplying medical details or promising to compensate through overexertion. In a real context, send or say it only to the appropriate person. For the fictional case, rehearse both acceptance and disappointment; acknowledge the other person's needs while retaining the limit.

Observable checks:

- The message specifies a current limit and valued purpose.
- A feasible alternative is offered.
- Unnecessary medical disclosure is avoided.
- The response does not create an obligation to overcompensate.

### 3. Use the flexible plan and review immediate and later effects

Follow the appropriate version during the ordinary day, or walk through the fictional day with this change: capacity is lower than expected before the conversation. Record which choice changed, what support was used and what happened to the valued purpose. For a personal trial, leave room to notice later effects before deciding the activity was sustainable; follow existing clinical advice and seek it for new or concerning changes. Do not increase a quota because one occasion went well. Revise one scheduling assumption or keep the supported version.

Observable checks:

- A changed-capacity situation is actually observed or explicitly simulated.
- The plan changes without abandoning the valued purpose by default.
- Immediate and delayed effects are distinguished where observed.
- Revision does not impose automatic activity increases.

**Adaptation.** Use one activity, spoken choices or a trusted helper if planning itself is burdensome. Rest and receiving help can be appropriate choices. Keep medication, diet, rehabilitation and activity limits with qualified care. Do not use a healthy-person challenge, push through worsening symptoms or impose graded increases as part of this exercise.

**Review.** Which part of the plan remained meaningful when capacity changed, and which assumption was too rigid? Keep any unobserved later effects unknown. Choose a sustainable revision or a question for the care team; ordinary planning cannot establish improved health.

**What the outcomes can look like:**

- **Supportive:** A person replaced a visit with an agreed short message, preserved established care and reviewed later effects before deciding whether the plan was sustainable.
- **Mixed:** The reduced activity fit the morning but its later effects were unclear, so the person kept that uncertainty and avoided increasing the commitment.
- **Contradictory:** A good morning was used to justify catching up on every task, or care was skipped to meet an activity target invented by the exercise.
- **Inconclusive:** A flexible schedule was written but neither an actual day nor the supplied changed-capacity scenario was examined.

**Reference support:**

- [Manage ME/CFS](https://www.cdc.gov/me-cfs/management/index.html) — ME/CFS can involve delayed symptom worsening after exertion; activity management must respect individual limits, and standard exercise advice for healthy people can be unsuitable. Disease-specific caution against a universal activity-increase rule, not a diagnosis or a pacing prescription for every illness. The exercise preserves existing qualified care and does not set activity doses or change treatment.

Minimum and full completion retain the existing action-count and explicit final-review rules. Neither means mastery of the competency.

## 06.05 — Prepare a pain-care conversation around sustainable participation

**Goal.** Distinguish pain treatment, management and adaptation, raise questions about uncertainty and feared activity, and prepare a sustainable participation plan with qualified care.

**How this represents the competency.** This is care-conversation preparation and review of an existing plan. It does not diagnose pain, prescribe movement, decide which pain is safe, or test tolerance through deliberate provocation.

**Before starting.** Use the linked MedlinePlus chronic-pain overview and a private note. Start with this fictional concern: a person wants to join a regular conversation, but pain and worry about a flare make planning difficult. If using a personal example, use an ordinary activity already covered by your care guidance; keep treatment instructions unchanged.

**Time and effort.** Two preparation sessions of about 15 minutes and one review after an ordinary permitted activity or the supplied fictional scenario.

### 1. Separate the pain experience from three different care goals

Explain three aims in your own words: cure would resolve an underlying problem where possible; management seeks to reduce symptoms or their effects; adaptation changes how a valued activity is done. They can coexist without a promise that pain will disappear. For the fictional concern, separate the reported pain, the feared consequence of joining the conversation and what remains medically unknown. Describe pacing as planning activity and rest around individually appropriate limits, not a rule to do more each time. Neither fear nor normal-looking test results establish that pain is imaginary or that an activity is safe.

Observable checks:

- Cure management and adaptation are distinguished.
- Reported pain feared consequences and uncertainty are separated.
- Pacing does not become an automatic increase rule.
- Pain is not treated as a measure of weakness or honesty.

### 2. Prepare questions that a qualified care conversation must answer

Make a short agenda with the valued activity, its practical difficulty and these questions: what needs further assessment; what existing activities and adaptations fit my situation; what changes should prompt contact; and when should the plan be reviewed? Include a question about fear of activity without asking a peer to decide whether to push through it. Identify the actual clinician or service to contact and any access difficulty. If there is no current care relationship, locate a qualified entry point rather than approving a plan yourself. Keep medication changes, tests and rehabilitation decisions outside this exercise.

Observable checks:

- The agenda connects care questions to a valued activity.
- Uncertainty and contact conditions have explicit questions.
- A qualified service or existing clinician is identified.
- The exercise does not approve treatment or activity limits.

### 3. Make the participation plan conditional on real care guidance

Using existing guidance, specify an allowed form of the ordinary activity, available support, the agreed way to pause or stop and the next review. For the fictional case, compare a shorter remote conversation with a visit and identify what the person and care team would need to decide before either is called suitable. A personal trial occurs only within existing guidance and ordinary capacity; record what participation was possible and any concern to take back to care. An unanswered care question means the plan remains a proposal. Do not generate pain or remove useful aids to test a claim about avoidance.

Observable checks:

- The plan identifies guidance or clearly states what is unapproved.
- Participation has an available supported form.
- Pause and review arrangements are explicit.
- Any actual result is distinguished from a fictional comparison.

**Adaptation.** Use a helper to prepare the agenda, reduce writing or stay with the fictional concern. Follow individual guidance for fatigue or delayed worsening as well as pain. New severe or concerning symptoms require appropriate medical help, not an experiment. No physical challenge or medication change is part of completion.

**Review.** What does the person want to participate in, which clinical question remains open, and what next contact or review is appropriate? Distinguish a useful care conversation from symptom relief, and an approved supported activity from an untested proposal.

**What the outcomes can look like:**

- **Supportive:** A person brought a specific participation goal to their existing care plan, used an already agreed shorter format and recorded a question for follow-up.
- **Mixed:** The agenda was useful but the activity limits were not yet clear, so the proposal remained unapproved instead of becoming a self-prescribed trial.
- **Contradictory:** Pain was dismissed as weak character, or the exercise was used to push through a feared activity without appropriate guidance.
- **Inconclusive:** The learner listed treatments but did not connect a valued activity to a qualified contact, a question or a conditional plan.

**Reference support:**

- [Manage ME/CFS](https://www.cdc.gov/me-cfs/management/index.html) — ME/CFS can involve delayed symptom worsening after exertion; activity management must respect individual limits, and standard exercise advice for healthy people can be unsuitable. Disease-specific caution against a universal activity-increase rule, not a diagnosis or a pacing prescription for every illness. The exercise preserves existing qualified care and does not set activity doses or change treatment.
- [Chronic Pain](https://medlineplus.gov/chronicpain.html) — Chronic pain can affect daily function and wellbeing, and care may involve different approaches and professional support. General pain literacy only; does not establish a cause, certify an activity as safe, prescribe a regimen or validate the original participation-planning exercise. Cure, management and adaptation are used as explanatory distinctions, not promised outcomes.

Minimum and full completion retain the existing action-count and explicit final-review rules. Neither means mastery of the competency.

## 06.06 — Translate one access barrier into a specific participation request

**Goal.** Use medical, social and identity perspectives to understand disability, request an appropriate accommodation, and define success by chosen participation rather than unsupported performance.

**How this represents the competency.** One access case illustrates distinct perspectives and a concrete request. It does not certify accessibility, determine legal eligibility, or assign a disability identity to anyone.

**Before starting.** Use a real event you want to access, or this supplied case: an advertised community talk mentions only stairs, long standing periods and spoken questions. A participant wants to understand the talk and contribute a question using a suitable format. Read the WHO disability overview. Do not simulate an impairment or remove an existing aid.

**Time and effort.** About 20 minutes to examine the case, one request or draft, and a short review of the response or supplied variation.

### 1. Examine the same barrier through three different perspectives

Write three short accounts. A medical perspective asks about relevant function, symptoms and individual care needs; a social perspective asks how stairs, standing and one communication format restrict participation; an identity perspective asks how the person understands and wishes to describe their own experience. Each can matter without one erasing the others. Identify the goal of understanding and asking a question, then choose one barrier to address. For a real case, use only information the affected person chooses to share; access needs do not authorize guessing a diagnosis or imposing a label.

Observable checks:

- Medical social and identity perspectives are distinguished.
- A specific environmental or participation barrier is selected.
- The person's chosen goal is explicit.
- No diagnosis or identity is assigned from an access need.

### 2. Write an access request with a concrete usable alternative

Use this structure: I would like to participate in the talk. The standing-only format prevents that for me; can seating be available with the same view and a way to submit a question? Replace the barrier and requested change with the actual need. Ask who can confirm the arrangement and by when, and identify what remains unknown rather than assuming a promise. Offer a suitable alternative such as equivalent remote participation if it genuinely serves the goal. Drafting is sufficient for the supplied case; a real request is sent only with the affected person's agreement. Local rights and formal documentation requirements need appropriate local guidance.

Observable checks:

- The request connects a barrier to a participation goal.
- The proposed change is specific enough to answer.
- Responsibility and confirmation timing are requested.
- Disclosure and any real contact remain the person's choice.

### 3. Evaluate the arrangement against the chosen function

Define success as access to the content and a usable way to ask a question, not standing for the entire event or appearing independent. Examine an actual response, or this variation: seating is offered but placed where the talk cannot be heard. State which barrier is reduced, what still blocks participation and what clarification is needed. If an appropriate arrangement is used, record the actual result; an organizer's assurance alone is not evidence that it worked. Retain the person's preferred description and consider whether another request or a different format is warranted.

Observable checks:

- Success is defined by chosen function and values.
- A response or supplied variation is actually evaluated.
- Partial access is distinguished from effective participation.
- Any live result is distinguished from an assurance or draft.

**Adaptation.** Dictate the request or use a chosen advocate while retaining the affected person's preferences. A person without a current access need can analyze the supplied case without pretending to have the experience. No disability simulation, forced disclosure, removal of accommodation or expensive purchase is required.

**Review.** Which perspective helped reveal the barrier, what would confirm a usable change, and what remains unresolved? Excellence can include skilled use of support; this exercise cannot decide another person's identity, legal entitlement or overall ability.

**What the outcomes can look like:**

- **Supportive:** A request specified seating with a usable view and question format; the response was checked against those functions rather than mere attendance.
- **Mixed:** Seating was available but the audio was inaccessible, so the arrangement was recognized as partial and a further change was requested.
- **Contradictory:** The person was told to prove commitment by managing without an aid, or participation was celebrated while the actual access barrier remained.
- **Inconclusive:** The learner endorsed inclusion in general but identified neither a concrete barrier nor a request someone could answer.

**Reference support:**

- [Disability](https://www.who.int/health-topics/disability) — Disability involves interaction between health conditions and personal or environmental factors; inaccessible environments and negative attitudes can obstruct participation. Conceptual and inclusion support only. It does not assign an identity, establish a local legal entitlement or prove that a proposed accommodation works for a particular person.

Minimum and full completion retain the existing action-count and explicit final-review rules. Neither means mastery of the competency.

## 06.07 — Design a cognitive support around an observed task difficulty

**Goal.** Recognize variation in attention, sensory processing, communication, executive function, learning and regulation, then test a support suited to a specific task without imposing a diagnostic stereotype.

**How this represents the competency.** A small task comparison examines whether a support is useful in that context. It does not diagnose neurodivergence, measure intelligence or require suppressing harmless differences to look typical.

**Before starting.** Use the supplied instruction: choose a time for a short catch-up, identify one question to discuss, and note how a change of plan will be communicated. Use an existing comfortable note or communication tool. Read the W3C cognitive-accessibility reference for examples of varied needs and design barriers. No diagnosis or disclosure is required.

**Time and effort.** About 15 minutes to map the task and two short ordinary uses with a 10-minute comparison.

### 1. Locate the obstacle before choosing a label or solution

Examine six task demands: attention involves keeping the relevant step in view; sensory processing concerns distracting or uncomfortable input; social communication concerns interpreting an unclear request; executive function concerns starting, ordering and switching steps; learning concerns understanding and retaining the method; regulation concerns noticing overload and taking an appropriate pause. For each, record an observed difficulty, a useful support already present or unknown. Choose one specific mismatch, such as losing the next step in a long message. Do not infer an entire profile from a single difficulty or assume that a diagnostic label predicts everyone's strengths and needs.

Observable checks:

- Six cognitive task demands are considered.
- Observation and unknowns remain distinct.
- One specific task mismatch is chosen.
- A difficulty is not turned into a diagnosis or global deficit.

### 2. Create a support that changes the task rather than judging the person

For the default mismatch, rewrite the instruction as three short entries labeled time, question and change of plan; add a visible next-step marker. If another mismatch was actually observed, choose one suitable alternative: reduce an unwanted notification, offer text instead of ambiguous speech, supply an example, or allow an agreed pause. State what the support is expected to make easier and keep other helpful supports in place. Do not aim for compulsory eye contact, stillness, faster work or concealment of an identity. If helping someone else, ask which change they want instead of assigning it from a label.

Observable checks:

- The support addresses the selected mismatch.
- Its intended practical effect is stated.
- Existing useful supports are preserved.
- Affected people choose rather than receive a stereotyped intervention.

### 3. Try the supported format and look for cost as well as benefit

## 06.08 — Agree on help that preserves consent during reduced capacity

**Goal.** Accept appropriate care during a season of reduced agency while communicating preferences and participating in decisions through an accessible form.

**How this represents the competency.** One ordinary help agreement practices receiving care and retaining a voice. A rehearsal does not establish experience of illness, injury, grief, unemployment, aging or crisis. It is not a legal capacity assessment or medical consent form.

For the case, the person chooses items; the helper may explain or enter agreed delivery options; payment credentials remain private. Observable checks:

- Decision choices and practical assistance are separated.
- Privacy preferences are explicit.
- Yes no uncertainty and pause have usable communication forms.
- Reduced capacity is not automatically treated as loss of decision authority.

### 2. Negotiate a bounded agreement that can be changed

Use this opening: I would like help with the delivery options; I want to choose the items, and please check before changing anything. Observable checks:

- The agreement identifies task timing and helper limits.
- Help is limited to what was agreed.
- A changed preference prompts a fresh check.
- Backup arrangements do not depend on coercion or unlimited access.

### 3. Use the agreement and check whose decision actually prevailed

Resolve it through the person's accessible communication and the helper's genuine practical limits, not by assuming dependence settles the disagreement. Observable checks:

- An actual exchange or explicitly labeled variation is examined.
- The person's stated preference affects the outcome.
- Privacy and the option to pause remain effective.
- Unmet essential needs lead to appropriate support rather than blame.

**Adaptation.** Use accessible yes/no options, extra processing time, an interpreter or a chosen advocate without bypassing the person. No one is required to provide intimate care, disclose credentials or surrender an existing support. If decision-making ability is genuinely uncertain, use qualified local guidance instead of a peer verdict.

## 06.10 — Preserve dignity and connection when a valued role changes

**Goal.** Separate human worth from independence, productivity, cognition, appearance and usefulness, then choose conduct that sustains self-respect and relationship under limitation.

**How this represents the competency.** The exercise examines a changed-role situation and one relational action. It does not require feeling positive about loss or prove adaptation to a permanent disability or cognitive change.

**Before starting.** Use this fictional case: a former community organizer can no longer manage events and fears being welcome only when useful. A small manageable personal role change is optional. Use a private note or spoken reflection; no painful personal history or public statement is required.

**Time and effort.** About 15 minutes for the distinction, one modest relational step or rehearsal, and a 10-minute review.

### 1. Identify five conditions that must not become a price of belonging

Consider five mistaken conditions: being independent enough, producing enough, remembering or reasoning quickly enough, looking a certain way, and being useful to others. For each, name a concrete respectful response that does not depend on meeting the condition: asking how help is wanted, including someone without an assigned job, allowing communication time, respecting appearance preferences, or offering company without repayment. In the case, distinguish the real loss of organizing ability from the unsupported claim that the person no longer deserves welcome. Let sadness or anger about the loss remain.

Observable checks:

- Five conditional judgments of worth are examined.
- Each is paired with a concrete respectful response.
- Actual loss is distinguished from loss of human worth.
- Difficult feelings are not treated as a failed attitude.

### 2. Offer or request connection without requiring a replacement achievement

Choose a low-demand form of contact with a suitable person, or draft this for the fictional organizer: I would like to stay in touch as a participant; I cannot run the event, but I would welcome a short conversation if it suits us both. The other person's response can offer genuine company without immediately assigning a new useful role. Agree on timing, form and a way to decline or end the exchange. An actual message is sent only in an appropriate relationship; fictional drafting remains rehearsal. No one must earn connection by telling an inspiring story or displaying cheerful endurance.

Observable checks:

- The proposed connection does not depend on a productive role.
- Limits and the desired form of contact are explicit.
- Both people can freely decline or end the exchange.
- Actual contact is distinguished from a fictional draft.

### 3. Check whether respect survives the absence of improvement

Review the actual exchange, or add this variation: the fictional organizer's limitation persists and communication takes longer than before. Ask whether the same welcome, privacy and opportunity to express preferences still apply. Identify any subtle bargain such as inclusion only if the person becomes useful again, improves quickly or hides their needs. Revise one phrase or arrangement that makes dignity conditional. Choose a sustainable way to remain connected, including supported communication or quiet company, while allowing grief and realistic limits to coexist.

Observable checks:

- Continuing limitation is considered without a recovery requirement.
- A conditional bargain is identified or explicitly checked for.
- One phrase or arrangement is reviewed against equal dignity.
- Connection can continue through supported or nonproductive forms.

**Adaptation.** Stay with the fictional case, use a trusted reader or express the choices through a familiar communication aid. **Review.** What real loss needs acknowledgment, and what respect or relationship should not depend on recovering it? Identify one changed interaction or an unresolved need for support. No response to this exercise measures human worth.

- **Mixed:** A warm invitation still assumed the person would resume organizing soon; the expectation was named and removed.

## 06.11 — Prepare for a setback without erasing earlier progress

**Goal.** Recognize fluctuating capacity and recurrence, and maintain a practical plan for support, reduced demands and a considered return that respects existing care.

**How this represents the competency.** This is a recurrence-plan exercise, not a relapse prediction tool or treatment protocol. It does not prescribe substance withdrawal, medication changes or a fixed recovery schedule.

**Time and effort.** About 20 minutes to prepare and one short later check of the plan's usability; no setback needs to occur.

### 1. Read the changing record without turning it into an all-or-nothing verdict

Separate three observations: the earlier routine happened with support, current capacity has changed, and the reason or duration of the change is not yet established. Distinguish a missed commitment, a familiar fluctuation already addressed in care and a new or concerning change that needs qualified advice. Do not label them interchangeable relapses. Identify an existing helpful support that can remain useful even when independent activity is reduced.

Observable checks:

- Earlier and current observations are both preserved.
- An unknown cause or duration is not invented.
- Ordinary setbacks familiar fluctuations and concerning changes are distinguished.
- A useful continuing support is identified.

### 2. Write a recurrence plan with concrete actions and contacts

Make four entries: changes to notice under existing guidance; demands that can be reduced; a willing support person or qualified service; and how to revisit a valued routine. Observable checks:

- The plan names observable changes without diagnosing them.
- Reduced demands and practical support are specific.
- A real support route and unavailable-contact alternative are recorded.

Test the plan with this change: the usual helper is unavailable and the next optional commitment is tomorrow. **What the outcomes can look like:**

- **Supportive:** The plan retained evidence of earlier participation, offered an alternative helper and allowed a supported return based on current capacity.

## 06.14 — Prepare a clear handoff when a concern exceeds self-help

**Goal.** Recognize the limits of peer and self-guided support across trauma, suicidality, abuse, psychosis, addiction, severe functional decline and medical concerns, and identify appropriate professional or emergency routes.

**How this represents the competency.** This is resource preparation and fictional response rehearsal, not diagnosis, a risk score, emergency-response certification or a substitute for contacting help in a real situation.

**Time and effort.** About 20 minutes to locate resources and a short private handoff rehearsal; no real emergency or test call is needed.

### 1. Recognize seven situations in which the curriculum is insufficient

Make seven short entries: persistent trauma-related distress needs a qualified assessment rather than forced retelling; suicidal thoughts need prompt crisis or professional help rather than a promise to cope alone; suspected abuse needs appropriate protection support; new experiences such as hearing things others do not or losing touch with reality need prompt clinical assessment; substance dependence or withdrawal concerns need qualified treatment advice; marked decline in basic functioning needs medical or clinical evaluation; and concerning physical symptoms need appropriate medical care. These are reasons to seek help, not diagnoses or an exhaustive list of emergencies. If danger is immediate or life-threatening, use emergency services; uncertainty is a reason to ask a professional, not to award a low-risk label.

Observable checks:

- All seven categories have an appropriate boundary explanation.
- The entries direct to qualified help without making diagnoses.
- Immediate danger is separated from a routine appointment process.
- Uncertainty does not become a claim that the situation is safe.

### 2. Build a small resource card that works in the relevant location

Record the official local emergency number, a crisis-support route, an ordinary clinical entry point and an appropriate safeguarding or abuse-support route. For the US starting example, verify 911 and 988 through the NIMH reference, then locate the actual local clinical and protection services. Check location served, contact method, availability and any accessibility or eligibility information; leave unknown details explicit. Include an alternative when an ordinary service is closed or unreachable. Save public contact information where it is accessible; do not call emergency or crisis lines just to test them or submit a fictional report.

Observable checks:

- Four types of help have location-appropriate public routes.
- Availability accessibility and eligibility are checked or marked unknown.
- An unavailable-service alternative is recorded.
- Verification uses public information without a test emergency contact.

### 3. Rehearse a brief handoff without trying to become the clinician

Privately rehearse: I am concerned because of this observed change. I can help contact the appropriate service; this needs more than our exercise. Supply only a plain observation, not a diagnosis, and ask how to make contact in the person's usable communication form. Compare a nonurgent care appointment, a suicidal statement needing prompt crisis support and an immediate life-threatening medical situation needing emergency help. Do not promise secrecy, argue someone out of an unusual belief, investigate abuse or improvise treatment. In a real event, use the appropriate service promptly and follow trained guidance while keeping your own safety in view; never wait to collect exercise evidence.

Observable checks:

- The handoff states an observation and a concrete help route.
- Three scenarios receive distinct appropriate contact responses.
- Privacy is respected without promising absolute secrecy.
- No treatment investigation or delay for evidence is introduced.

**Adaptation.** Prepare text or communication-aid wording and a locally appropriate accessible contact route. A trusted helper may assist with public information. Keep the rehearsal fictional and brief. Contact details and service scope can change, so check the card before relying on it later; actual urgency takes precedence over this practice.

**Review.** Can you find the right kind of help and communicate a concern without diagnosing or delaying? Identify an unresolved access detail and a realistic way to clarify it. Completing a card or rehearsal does not qualify someone to manage a crisis alone.

**What the outcomes can look like:**

- **Supportive:** The card contained verified local routes and the rehearsal distinguished an ordinary appointment from prompt crisis contact and a life-threatening emergency.
- **Mixed:** The emergency route was clear but the preferred crisis contact had an unresolved communication-access question, which remained explicitly open.
- **Contradictory:** A peer labeled a situation safe without assessment, promised secrecy, or delayed seeking help until the curriculum exercise was complete.
- **Inconclusive:** The learner listed professional titles without usable contact routes or a concrete handoff for the supplied scenarios.

**Reference support:**

- [Help for Mental Illnesses](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/find-help) — US life-threatening emergencies call for 911; 988 provides suicide and emotional-crisis support by call or text, and ordinary mental health care requires an appropriate provider. US public resource guidance only. Local availability, accessibility, eligibility and other countries' services need current verification. A resource card or rehearsal is not a clinical assessment or emergency-response qualification.

Minimum and full completion retain the existing action-count and explicit final-review rules. Neither means mastery of the competency.
