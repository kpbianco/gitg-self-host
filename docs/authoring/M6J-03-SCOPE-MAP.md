# Competency scope review: spirituality, mortality and hope

The requirement is individually written, self-contained competency content:
understandable without another competency page or a personally designed
exercise, representative of the canonical goal, and adaptable without
assuming a particular person's identity or circumstances. The action numbers
below refer to the corresponding entry in `exercises/03.yaml` or
`exercises/04.yaml`. The compiled canonical practice is the runtime source.

This is an explicit authoring map, not an automated semantic verdict or a
completed specialist review. A listed action must actually contain the work
described here. Named alternatives are legitimate pathways; a person does
not have to perform incompatible beliefs, every kind of loss or every
religious rite to demonstrate a meaningful attempt. Rehearsal and preparation
retain their narrower claims.

| ID and canonical competency | Essential scope represented in the actions | Evidence and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 03.01 Metaphysical and spiritual inquiry | A1 compares an explicit pair or range of positions for each of God, consciousness, soul, freedom, providence and transcendence. A2 compares religious, spiritual, philosophical and naturalistic accounts with reasons and difficulties, plus an actual primary account. A3 states position or principled uncertainty, objection and revision condition. | A retrievable comparison and reasoned conclusion. Six questions are mapped; one is examined more deeply. Neither subjective intensity nor agreement is proof. |
| 03.02 Serious study of a wisdom tradition | A1 supplies primary texts and historical ordering. A2 connects an exemplar with a teaching, corrects an interpretation and tries a practice. A3 examines a contemporary community, an internal interpretive difference and a caricature. | One coherent account contains text, history, practice, exemplar, differences and community. No source access means source study remains unverified. A small unit does not stand in for an entire tradition. |
| 03.03 Contemplation, prayer and silence | A1 defines a repeatable sequence with prayer, reading, silence or attentive presence as chosen forms. A2 observes actual occasions. A3 examines honesty, attention, gratitude, resolve and peace without requiring all five effects. | A modest pattern with missed and difficult occasions preserved. No promise of reliable improvement from five observations and no clinical outcome claim. |
| 03.04 Reverence and the sacred | A1 names life, nature, persons, ritual, truth, beauty and the holy as alternatives, then specifies respectful conduct. A2 separates experience, interpretation, evidence and conscience. A3 makes care extend beyond the encounter and respects outsiders. | An actual encounter and related action. Awe need not occur; reverence does not authorize interference with a person or tradition. |
| 03.05 Gratitude and received life | A1 traces dependence, gifts, contingency, sacrifice and inheritance while retaining personal effort. A2 responds through care, generosity, remembrance or useful service. A3 revisits the response during a later use. | A traced good and observable conduct. Gratitude does not create unlimited debt, erase harm or require contact with an unsafe person. |
| 03.06 Surrender and limits of control | A1 separates choice, influence, uncertainty, finitude and dependence. A2 acts responsibly and respects an outcome boundary. A3 reviews action independently from success or failure. | A real wait and action, or explicitly narrower fictional analysis. Acceptance is compatible with advocacy and necessary care. |
| 03.07 Religious and spiritual community | A1 examines shared practice, care, service, celebration and accountability. A2 makes an agreed contribution and joins or observes a permitted practice. A3 compares stated values with conduct and the treatment of a boundary. | Live contribution is distinct from researching a group. One welcome does not certify safety or establish lasting belonging. |
| 03.08 Vocation and service beyond self | A1 connects a chosen truth, faith, community, craft, cause or future good to a real need, promise and cost. A2 delivers useful work. A3 checks benefit, cost and continuation. | Actual time and effort serve a specified use. Vocation need not mean employment or permanent identity; sacrifice does not justify neglecting essential duties. |
| 03.09 Ritual, remembrance and sacred time | A1 distinguishes beginnings, endings, grief, gratitude, rest, commitment, repentance, celebration and belonging, then maps the chosen sequence to purpose. A2 performs it. A3 checks resulting action and a second observance. | Symbol, memory, identity and conduct remain linked. No requirement to introduce grief, use restricted forms or imitate another community's rite. |
| 03.10 Communal worship, liturgy and shared sacred practice | A1 identifies tradition/shared story, meaning, leadership and eligibility. A2 takes a sincere permitted role in worship, recitation, silence or remembrance. A3 links communal meaning to ordinary conduct. | Live participation and observation remain distinct. The supplied shared remembrance is named as newly chosen, not falsely attributed to an established tradition. Sacramental or pilgrimage participation is never imposed. |
| 03.11 Authority, tradition, conscience and reform | A1 differentiates texts, teachers, institutions, inherited practice, experience and conscience. A2 examines legitimate coordination, coercion and accountability. A3 gives conditions for compliance, dissent, review, reform and exit, then reassesses a changed case. | A defensible decision process with missing facts intact. Fictional discernment does not adjudicate abuse or authorize confrontation. |
| 03.12 Religious pluralism and interfaith humility | A1 uses two actual self-descriptions and preserves a real disagreement. A2 rehearses or conducts a voluntary respectful exchange. A3 tests cooperation against refusal, belief discussion and truthful participation. | Source accuracy, real disagreement and workable shared conduct. Rehearsal never claims live cooperation; people are not assigned beliefs from labels. |
| 03.13 Mystical, ecstatic and peak experience | A1 distinguishes awe, unity, devotion, revelation and transcendence from interpretation. A2 introduces reason and a willing outside perspective or stated fictional comparison. A3 tests ordinary conduct and revisits meaning over time. | Integration without inducing an experience or granting intensity automatic truth. The fictional event is not claimed as personal experience; destabilizing experiences require suitable support. |
| 04.01 Suffering, evil and lament | A1 acknowledges pain, injustice, tragedy, apparent meaninglessness and unanswered questions, including prayer by choice. A2 practices acknowledgment without denial. A3 acts on a need while leaving explanations open. | Honest lament and actual ordinary care. No forced lesson, preferred feeling or theological answer; no exposure to acute personal suffering. |
| 04.02 Mortality and impermanence | A1 examines changing bodies, relationships, possessions, achievements, aging and loss without a predicted death date. A2 chooses a priority or safe relational repair with a real tradeoff. A3 carries it out. | A changed allocation of finite time. Reconciliation is voluntary; rest is a legitimate priority; no urgency to maximize every moment. |
| 04.03 Tragic knowledge and limits of control | A1 preserves good claims and unavoidable residual costs. A2 gives a fair process and mitigation without erasing loss. A3 applies the distinction between disappointment and meaninglessness in an ordinary choice. | Defensible reasoning and a modest real trial. No fabricated lossless answer, victim blame or claim to decide high-consequence allocations. |
| 04.04 Meaning after catastrophic loss | A1 maps what ended, changed, remains and is unknown across practical, relational and identity effects. A2 matches needs with appropriate support. A3 tries one sustaining commitment. | Preparation or a manageable personal next step, not demonstrated recovery from bereavement, betrayal, disaster, illness or displacement. A new orientation need not be a redemptive story. |
| 04.05 Absurdity, existential anxiety and meaninglessness | A1 separates feeling empty, repetition, uncertainty and a total nihilistic inference. A2 acts from a chosen local responsibility or genuine faith commitment. A3 reviews practical consequence and mood separately. | Conduct can have a defensible reason while ultimate questions remain open. The exercise does not diagnose or treat depression. |
| 04.06 Endurance without self-optimization | A1 defines enough care with patience, lament, company, rest and necessary responsibilities. A2 observes ordinary occasions without an improvement quota. A3 retains care and reduces unnecessary demands. | Humane presence in an unresolved period. No streak, suffering challenge or requirement to refuse help; quiet care is not a productivity project. |
| 04.07 Moral injury and compromised action | A1 covers acting, witnessing, not preventing and betrayal; separates guilt, shame, trauma, responsibility, repair and support. A2 assigns questions to suitable roles. A3 locates an actual support route and protects private information. | Literacy and support preparation using a fictional case. No diagnosis, culpability determination, trauma processing, forced confession or presumed repair. |
| 04.08 Dying, end-of-life choices and accompaniment | A1 prepares care values, decision support and local questions about formal documents and palliative care. A2 rehearses presence, grief, privacy, unfinished relationships and dignity without false reassurance. A3 takes a preparation step and preserves revisability. | A conversation plan and respectful accompaniment rehearsal. No executed directive, treatment choice, legal appointment or claim of actual accompaniment from the exercise alone. |
| 04.09 Hope without denial | A1 holds adverse evidence and reasons for grief alongside possibilities. A2 acts from relationship, faith, meaning or care without guaranteeing an outcome. A3 updates expectations when evidence changes. | A useful act and revisable hope. Outcome hope and hope about one's response are distinct; adverse probabilities are not invented or hidden. |
| 04.10 Irreversibility, remorse and living after the unfixable | A1 separates permanent consequence, remaining responsibility and others' choices, including missed opportunity and incomplete restitution. A2 defines appropriate repair and prevention. A3 reviews continued service and learning without claiming absolution. | Responsible conduct with permanent loss acknowledged. Forgiveness is not purchased, a fictional apology is not delivered repair and self-punishment is not a required action. |

The shared acceptance standard also requires a usable default, materials and
prerequisites, ordered instructions, four observable checks per action,
supportive/mixed/contradictory/inconclusive examples, adaptations, realistic
burden and a specific review. All 23 entries provide those fields. Whether
these instructions are effective, accessible across populations or acceptable
to qualified specialists remains separate work.

The remaining 306 competencies are still rewrite-pending. The earlier 54
authored entries also remain subject to semantic and specialist review; this
map does not retrospectively certify them.
