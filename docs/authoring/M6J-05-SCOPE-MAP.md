# M6J-05: emotional maturity scope-to-task review

This map connects every domain 07 canonical scope and evidence-of-progress
goal to the individually authored actions in `exercises/07.yaml`. A1–A3 refer
to the ordered actions in the complete practice reader. These are bounded
educational exercises pending human acceptance, not therapy or an assessment
of total emotional maturity. Canonical names are preserved below even where
the executable practice has a narrower title.

| Competency | How the canonical scope is exercised | Observable result and remaining limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 07.01 Emotional vocabulary and differentiation | A1 separates event/trigger, emotion, intensity, bodily sensation, meaning and urge. A2 explicitly compares anger, hurt, shame, fear, envy, grief and exhaustion with reasons and uncertainty. A3 tests whether the distinction changes a response and repeats the description. | Two differentiated accounts and a concern-specific response. A fictional person's feelings remain hypotheses; vocabulary is not proof of accurate interpretation or regulation. |
| 07.02 Body-based emotional awareness | A1 considers breathing, tension, temperature, energy, posture, pain and activation without forced detection. A2 records whether a cue precedes behavior and attempts an early practical response. A3 repeats the cue-response link or replaces an unreliable cue. | An observed sequence can establish earlier intervention on that occasion. External prompts preserve response choice but do not demonstrate increased internal body awareness. Symptoms are not diagnosed as emotion. |
| 07.03 The pause between feeling and action | A1 offers breath, external grounding, movement, delay, prayer, writing and consultation as legitimate alternatives and selects one with a return condition. A2 performs the pause before a consequential response. A3 returns to the feeling and issue with a request or repair. | A pause-and-return sequence, not suppression or indefinite avoidance. One selected method is sufficient; incompatible beliefs or inaccessible body practices are never required. |
| 07.04 Anxiety and courageous exposure | A1 chooses a worthwhile suitable step, orders manageable versions and predicts an observable feared outcome. A2 makes a real attempt with apprehension allowed. A3 repeats and compares actual outcomes before choosing any revision. | Two real ordinary attempts can inform a narrow fear prediction. A ladder or rehearsal alone cannot demonstrate exposure; severe symptoms, trauma and unsafe situations require a different support pathway. |
| 07.05 Anger and assertive repair | A1 distinguishes agreement/value violation, impact and ego threat. A2 removes escalation and states anger, facts and a request directly. A3 acknowledges personal harm and practices proportionate follow-through, including refusal. | A clear boundary or repair can coexist with unresolved anger and disagreement. Intimidation, suppression and passive aggression are explicitly contrasted with the actual statement. |
| 07.06 Sadness, grief, and loss | A1 names sorrow or other honest responses, what was lost, what remains and changed expectations. A2 performs chosen remembrance or ritual and articulates a support preference. A3 attempts a modest continuation carrying a named value. | Acknowledgment, support request and changed-day action are distinct. No prescribed stages, recovery deadline, compulsory positive meaning or claim that a new activity replaces the loss. |
| 07.07 Shame, guilt, and remorse | A1 checks responsibility for specific conduct separately from total self-condemnation. A2 offers and performs proportionate repair without demanding forgiveness. A3 installs a prevention change and checks later conduct when an opportunity occurs. | Responsibility leads to an observable correction or an explicit boundary where blame is unsupported. Future change remains untested without a later opportunity; remorse is not a total-character verdict. |
| 07.08 Envy, jealousy, and comparison | A1 examines threatened needs, insecurity, admiration, real or assumed scarcity and possessiveness in praise and friendship cases. A2 selects appreciation, self-directed boundary, learning or a chosen goal. A3 performs the selected step using a supplied sentence-revision task or appropriate live action. | Comparison produces a constructive action without surveillance or control. Remaining envy does not invalidate a useful choice; a private invitation draft is not an agreement. |
| 07.09 Healthy emotional expression | A1 supplies event, feeling, need and request while identifying leakage through withdrawal, hostility, performance or control. A2 asks a suitable listener for space and communicates proportionately. A3 checks understanding and limits any agreement to what is mutual. | A real disclosure and response can demonstrate clarity with that listener. A private rehearsal remains preparation; no risky disclosure is used to test trustworthiness. |
| 07.10 Self-compassion with standards | A1 joins care for suffering to specific accountability while rejecting indulgence and punishment. A2 defines a feasible legitimate standard, repair and remaining consequence. A3 attempts correction and compares actual work with that standard. | A humane internal response is assessed by whether it supports renewed action, not pleasant wording alone. Care does not erase consequences, and impossible obligations are not automatically accepted. |
| 07.11 Hope, optimism, joy, and contentment | A1 faces hardship and separates a realistic future possibility from a guarantee. A2 attends to an accessible present good, appreciation and enoughness without requiring joy. A3 attempts a bounded future step while retaining ambition and unresolved facts. | Present attention and realistic action can occur with sadness or neutrality. No compulsory optimism, denial of hardship or treatment claim follows. |
| 07.12 Resilience and post-setback adaptation | A1 separates facts, responsibility, constraints, unknowns, lessons and recovery needs. A2 changes an observed cause and identifies support. A3 retries against explicit criteria and retains unsuccessful results. | A supplied invitation task allows an actual redesign and retry. Better draft quality does not demonstrate attendance or resilience after major disruption; reasoned goal revision is allowed. |
| 07.13 Mental-health literacy and help-seeking | A1 uses functioning and safety cases to distinguish self-help limits and medical, psychological, spiritual, legal and emergency roles. A2 verifies local access details and fallbacks. A3 rehearses a request and locates urgent routes without test emergency calls; an actual need requires real contact. | A usable support card demonstrates preparation. Contact attempted, service reached and care received remain distinct; it is not a diagnosis, clinical risk assessment or crisis qualification. |
| 07.14 Chronic dysregulation and skill generalization | A1 defines two strategies, fit conditions, early cues and failure signs. A2 uses both in naturally different contexts. A3 selects a response in a third event and compares triggers, relationships, stress levels and environments, including where a method stops helping. | Three recorded ordinary events support only the observed range. Missing contexts stay unknown; severe or persistent dysregulation warrants care, not manufactured triggers or more intense self-help. |

Every package includes a supplied starting point, materials, burden,
observable action checks, specific adaptations, a review question and four
worked outcome dispositions. Default examples provide the task; choosing a
personal application does not require inventing a rubric. Fourteen practices
add 42 distinct action instructions, 168 checks and 56 outcome examples.

Authoring coverage becomes 105/383 with exactly 278 rewrite-pending. The other
369 canonical packages, all stable identities, parent mappings, activation,
assessment, scoring/ranking and explicit human closeout must remain unchanged.
The existing minimum/full completion rules concern bounded practice attempts,
not mastery or completion of every possible life context. Human semantic,
clinical, cultural, accessibility, measurement and empirical acceptance remain
open; this mapping records the authored design, not specialist sign-off.
