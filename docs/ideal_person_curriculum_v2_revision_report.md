# Ideal Person Curriculum v2 - Revision Report

## Bottom line

The audit-driven revision contains **27 domains** and **383 master competencies**.

The count was not selected in advance. It emerged from preserving the original 259 competencies, splitting overloaded domains, adding the missing modes identified by the audit, and retaining only competencies with distinct objects, failure modes, practices, or evidence.

This revision is designed to maximize conceptual completeness. It does **not** claim psychometric validation or a culture-free definition of perfect personhood. Those dimensions cannot honestly become 10/10 through editing alone; they require expert review, diverse pilots, fairness testing, and longitudinal outcomes.

## What changed

- Added a dedicated domain for mortality, tragedy, catastrophic loss, moral injury, and hope.
- Added a dedicated domain for trauma, disability, neurodiversity, chronic illness, dependence, and receiving care.
- Added a dedicated domain for digital life, AI, algorithmic feeds, synthetic media, privacy, and cybersecurity.
- Split intimacy and sexuality from family, parenting, reproduction, and caregiving.
- Split personal finance from entrepreneurship, markets, macroeconomics, and economic systems.
- Expanded civic life with propaganda resistance, media institutions, nonviolent action, civil disobedience, peacebuilding, and mutual aid.
- Expanded work with labor rights, exploitation, worker voice, accessibility, and boundaries.
- Expanded leadership with anti-corruption, institutional capture, whistleblowing, governance, and abuse response.
- Expanded spirituality with liturgy, religious authority and reform, interfaith humility, and mystical experience.
- Expanded play with communal celebration, pilgrimage, inclusive recreation, humor, and ecological reciprocity.
- Added statistics, scientific reasoning, causal inference, model limits, apprenticeship, and accessible learning.

## Structural corrections

- Human dignity is explicitly not scored.
- There is no recommended single 'perfect person' score.
- Task completion no longer implies trait mastery.
- Self-report alone cannot advance a competency score.
- Optional roles such as parenthood, leadership, partnership, entrepreneurship, or a specific religious path can be marked not applicable without penalty.
- Agency, receptivity, interdependence, adaptation, stewardship, and transcendence are all treated as legitimate modes of formation.
- Evidence quality, recency, context transfer, and contradiction are tracked separately from completion quantity.

## Honest scorecard

| Dimension | Structural status after revision | What still requires evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Breadth of human coverage | Designed for full-scope coverage | Expert gap review may still find omissions |
| Cross-tradition overlap | Explicit pluralist and tradition-aware architecture | Review by scholars and practitioners inside each tradition |
| Practical usefulness | Every competency retains scope and evidence fields | Tasks, sequencing, burden, and outcome testing with users |
| Taxonomic cleanliness | Distinct domains, uneven counts, applicability classes, and redundancy controls | Semantic review and real-user comprehension testing |
| Empirical measurability | Mixed-evidence architecture and anti-gaming rules included | Reliability, validity, and longitudinal outcome studies |
| Cultural universality | Cultural adaptation and multiple-pathway rules included | Differential-item and fairness testing across populations |
| Assessment readiness | Scoring policy, N/A handling, and evidence standards included | Psychometric calibration; it is not yet a validated assessment |

## Recommended use

Treat this YAML as the **content ontology and specification**, not as the user-facing list. The product should:

1. Determine role and pathway applicability.
2. Assess a small number of relevant domains using mixed evidence.
3. Select one or a few current competencies.
4. Surface concrete lessons, practices, and reflections beneath those competencies.
5. Reassess only after repeated real-world evidence.
6. Keep a transparent explanation of why a recommendation changed.

The full ontology should remain mostly invisible to a user at any one time.
